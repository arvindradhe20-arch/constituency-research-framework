# Constituency Research & Political Intelligence Framework

### A neutral, evidence-driven framework for constituency profiling, public-opinion research and political intelligence

**Prepared by Arvind Yadav**  
Political Research | Public Policy | Campaign Strategy | Data & Survey Analytics

---

## Overview

This repository presents a structured methodology for understanding an electoral constituency through **publicly available information, primary research, field observations, stakeholder mapping and survey data**.

The purpose is to demonstrate how fragmented information can be converted into a systematic constituency research product for political research, public-policy analysis and representative-office decision support.

> **Important:** This is a portfolio and methodology project. It does not contain confidential client information, personal voter data, proprietary campaign material or recommendations to vote for or against any political actor.

## Research Objectives

A constituency intelligence exercise should help a research team answer five broad questions:

1. **What does the constituency look like?**  geography, administrative structure, population and socio-economic context.
2. **What issues are residents reporting?**  recurring governance, infrastructure, livelihood and service-delivery concerns.
3. **Who are the relevant stakeholders?**  public institutions, elected representatives, civil-society actors, community organisations and other publicly identifiable stakeholders.
4. **What does available evidence show?**  election results, government data, surveys, field reports and publicly available sources.
5. **How should information be monitored?**  a repeatable reporting structure that distinguishes verified facts, field observations, public claims and unresolved questions.

## 01 — Constituency Profile

Create a concise baseline profile containing:

- Constituency name and type
- District and state
- Parliamentary/Assembly relationship
- Administrative units
- Urban/rural composition where reliable data is available
- Population and socio-economic indicators from authoritative sources
- Major economic activities
- Transport and connectivity context
- Key public institutions

Every factual field should include a **source and date** so that historical information is not presented as current information.

## 02 — Geographic & Administrative Mapping

The objective is to understand how the constituency is organised administratively and geographically.

Suggested fields:

| Unit | Parent Unit | Type | Population/Data Source | Key Public Facilities | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example Unit | Example Block | Ward/Village | Source + Year | School/PHC/etc. | Research note |

Geographic analysis should rely on legitimate public or project-authorised data and should avoid publishing sensitive household-level information.

## 03 — Socio-Economic Profile

Possible indicators include:

- Population and household indicators
- Education and literacy
- Employment and livelihoods
- Agriculture and local industry
- Health infrastructure
- Water and sanitation
- Roads and transport
- Digital connectivity
- Public-service access
- Relevant government-programme indicators

The analytical principle is simple: **indicator → source → date → comparison → research implication**.

## 04 — Electoral History

Historical election information should be recorded descriptively rather than converted into unsupported forecasts.

| Election | Year | Candidate | Party | Votes | Vote Share | Source |
|---|---:|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Assembly/Parliament | YYYY | Candidate | Party | — | — | Official source |

Useful analysis may document turnout, historical changes and publicly reported results while clearly separating historical evidence from current public opinion.

## 05 — Issue Mapping

Issue mapping converts unstructured observations into a research register.

### Issue Register

| Issue ID | Location | Issue Category | Description | Evidence/Source | Date Reported | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I-001 | Example | Roads | Example description | Field/Public source | YYYY-MM-DD | Verification pending |

Recommended categories can include infrastructure, employment, agriculture, health, education, public services, local administration and other constituency-specific concerns.

### Verification Standard

Each issue should be marked as one of:

- **Verified fact** — supported by a reliable source or direct documented evidence
- **Field observation** — observed/reported during structured field research
- **Stakeholder claim** — attributed to the person/organisation making the claim
- **Public/media report** — attributed to the relevant publication/source
- **Unverified input** — requires additional validation

This prevents assumptions from being presented as established facts.

## 06 — Stakeholder Mapping

The framework maps **roles and institutions**, not private political preferences.

Possible stakeholder categories:

- Elected representatives
- Local-government institutions
- Government departments
- Civil-society organisations
- Community organisations
- Trade/business associations
- Educational institutions
- Publicly identifiable subject-matter experts
- Media organisations

### Stakeholder Register

| Stakeholder/Institution | Category | Public Role | Geography | Relevant Issue Area | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Example | Institution | Public function | Area | Education | Public source |

Sensitive personal data and inferred political preferences should not be collected or published.

## 08 — Public Opinion & Survey Analytics

A survey module can measure attitudes toward public issues and governance priorities when designed with appropriate sampling and privacy safeguards.

### Questionnaire Structure

1. Respondent eligibility and broad non-sensitive profile
2. Local issue awareness
3. Priority public-service concerns
4. Experience with selected public services
5. Governance/policy awareness where relevant
6. Open-ended feedback

### Survey Quality Checklist

- Define target population before fieldwork
- Document sampling method
- Record sample size
- Avoid leading questions
- Use consistent question wording
- Document non-response where possible
- Conduct quality-control checks
- Protect respondent privacy
- Report limitations with every analysis

Survey findings should never be presented as representative of the entire constituency unless the sampling design supports that conclusion.

## 09 — Media & Public Information Monitoring

A structured tracker can consolidate publicly available developments.

| Date | Topic | Source | Geography | Summary | Verification Status | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| YYYY-MM-DD | Example | Public source | Area | Short factual summary | Verified/Pending | Research |

Potential sources include official government releases, Election Commission material, legislative records, credible news reporting and other transparent public sources.

## 10 — Intelligence Reporting

### Weekly Research Brief

**A. Major developments**  
What changed during the week?

**B. Constituency issues**  
What issues were documented and what evidence supports them?

**C. Field observations**  
What was observed or reported during field research?

**D. Public-information developments**  
Relevant government, institutional or media updates.

**E. Survey/research findings**  
New findings, with methodology and limitations.

**F. Open questions**  
What still requires verification?

**G. Research priorities for next week**  
What should the research team investigate next?

## Research Principles

**Accuracy over speed.** A useful political-intelligence system must distinguish evidence from assumptions.

**Source everything.** Important factual claims should be traceable to a source and date.

**Separate fact from interpretation.** Field observations, stakeholder claims and analyst interpretations should be clearly labelled.

**Protect privacy.** Do not publish sensitive personal data or infer private political preferences.

**Report uncertainty.** Missing data, sampling limitations and conflicting evidence should be visible in the final analysis.

**Maintain neutrality in research.** The framework is designed to understand constituencies and public concerns, not to tell citizens how to vote.
