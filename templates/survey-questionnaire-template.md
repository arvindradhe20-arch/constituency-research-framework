## Section A — Eligibility / Broad Profile
Use only variables necessary for the research purpose and avoid unnecessary sensitive personal information.

1. Are you eligible for this study under the defined sampling criteria?
2. Broad age band (if methodologically required)
3. Broad location/administrative unit
4. Occupation/livelihood category (if relevant)

## Section B — Local Issues
5. What are the most important public issues affecting your area?
6. Which public service requires the most attention?
7. Have you personally experienced this issue? (Yes / No / Prefer not to say)

## Section C — Public Services
8. How would you describe access to selected public services relevant to this study?
9. What improvement would make the biggest difference to your locality?

## Section D — Awareness
10. Are you aware of relevant public programmes/services being studied?
11. If yes, what has been your experience accessing them?

## Section E — Open Feedback
12. What is one issue you would most like your public representatives or administration to understand about your area?

## Quality-Control Checklist
- Define the target population before data collection.
- Document the sampling approach.
- Avoid leading or double-barrelled questions.
- Keep question wording consistent.
- Protect respondent privacy.
- Document sample size, fieldwork dates and limitations.
- Do not claim constituency-wide representativeness unless the sampling design supports it.
